\section{Source Code}
\label{app:source}

This appendix documents how the source is organized and how densely it is
commented. The complete listing is not reproduced here: the archive submitted
with the report contains \code{src/}, \code{include/}, \code{tests/}, and
\code{tools/} in full, and the module map below identifies what each file
implements. The code is C++17 and builds with CMake.

\subsection{Comment Coverage}

Comment coverage is counted on the twelve core files (6,651 total lines, 6,112
non-blank lines). A line is \emph{comment-only} if its first non-space
characters begin a line or block comment; a line is \emph{comment-bearing} if it
contains a comment, whether or not it also contains code. Table~\ref{tab:comments}
reports both conventions instead of choosing the more favorable one.

\begin{table}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{Comment coverage of the C++ core.}
  \label{tab:comments}
  \footnotesize
  \begin{tabular}{l r r r r r}
    \toprule
    Scope & Non-blank & Comment-only & Comment-bearing &
      Comment-only & Comment-bearing \\
    \midrule
    \code{src/} & 5\,180 & 1\,304 & 1\,420 & 25.2\% & 27.4\% \\
    \code{include/} & 932 & 414 & 519 & 44.4\% & 55.7\% \\
    Combined & 6\,112 & 1\,718 & 1\,941 & 28.1\% & 31.7\% \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \par\smallskip
  \footnotesize Note: 223 lines contain both code and a comment; they are
  included in the comment-bearing column and excluded from the comment-only
  column. Measured against code-carrying lines alone, comment-only lines are
  39.1\%.
\end{table}

The combined core is 31.7\% comment-bearing; comment-only lines are 28.1\%.
Headers are commented more densely than implementation files, which is
expected: the headers state the invariants and ownership rules that the
implementation assumes. The requirement that comments make up at least 30\% of
the code is met under the comment-bearing convention; the comment-only figure
is reported as well so that the counting convention is visible. Comment
density is not a substitute for the tests in Chapter~\ref{sec:results}: the
cross-checks, not the comment ratio, are what establish correctness.
